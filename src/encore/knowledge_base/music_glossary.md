# Encore music glossary

These definitions are original wording written for this project. They describe
how listeners commonly use genre, mood, and catalog terms; they are not rigid
rules for classifying every recording.

## Ambient

Ambient music emphasizes atmosphere, sustained tone, and spatial texture more
than a foreground melody or driving beat. It can support quiet attention without
demanding constant focus.

## B-side

A B-side is the secondary song paired with a single, historically on the reverse
side of a physical record. Fans often use the term for non-album companion tracks
even when no literal two-sided disc exists.

## Bedroom pop

Bedroom pop describes intimate, self-produced pop whose small-room recording
character is part of its appeal. Soft vocals and handmade production are common,
but professional equipment does not disqualify the style.

## Breakbeat

Breakbeat uses syncopated drum patterns rather than an uninterrupted four-on-the-
floor pulse. The broken rhythmic accents create bounce and forward instability.

## Chillwave

Chillwave blends hazy synthesizers, softened drums, and nostalgic processing.
The mood often feels like a faded summer memory rather than sharply detailed pop.

## Deep cut

A deep cut is a lesser-known catalog track rather than an artist's familiar hit.
It is useful when a listener wants discovery without leaving a favorite artist.

## Dream pop

Dream pop puts melody inside soft-focus guitars, synthesizers, reverb, and airy
vocals. Its songs are usually clearer and gentler than the denser edge of
shoegaze.

## Drone

Drone music develops slowly around sustained pitches or tone clusters. Small
changes in texture matter more than conventional chord progressions or hooks.

## Dub

Dub reshapes reggae recordings through bass emphasis, echo, reverb, dropouts,
and studio mixing used as a compositional instrument.

## Electro

Electro centers on drum-machine rhythms, synthetic bass, and a sharply robotic
funk vocabulary. It is distinct from the broad everyday use of "electronic."

## Emo

Emo is guitar-based music associated with emotionally direct writing and dynamic
contrast. Different eras range from underground post-hardcore to polished pop.

## Focus music

Focus music is a listening function rather than one strict genre: steady,
low-distraction sound chosen to support concentration. Instrumentals, restrained
dynamics, and predictable textures often work well.

## Footwork

Footwork is fast electronic dance music built from clipped samples, restless
sub-bass, and complex rhythms associated with Chicago dance culture.

## Four-on-the-floor

Four-on-the-floor means a kick drum lands on every beat in common time. The
regular pulse anchors many disco, house, and techno tracks.

## Funk

Funk organizes the arrangement around interlocking rhythm, syncopated bass, and
short accented gestures. Groove normally carries more weight than harmonic
complexity.

## Glitch

Glitch music treats clicks, skips, digital errors, and fragmented audio as useful
musical material instead of defects to hide.

## Grime

Grime combines sparse electronic production, quick rhythmic patterns, heavy
bass, and forceful MC delivery. It developed in early-2000s London.

## Hypnagogic pop

Hypnagogic pop filters remembered commercial music through warped, dreamlike
production. It resembles a half-recalled broadcast more than clean nostalgia.

## IDM

IDM is a contested label for electronic music that foregrounds unusual sound
design and intricate rhythms, often aimed at close listening as much as dancing.

## Indie folk

Indie folk combines acoustic songwriting with the production values and personal
scale associated with independent rock. Arrangements can range from bare voice
and guitar to chamber-like ensembles.

## Jangle pop

Jangle pop features bright, ringing guitars, concise melodies, and a light rhythmic
feel. Twelve-string guitar tones are a common reference point.

## Krautrock

Krautrock is an umbrella term for experimental West German rock of the late 1960s
and 1970s. Repetition, electronics, improvisation, and the motorik pulse recur
across otherwise varied artists.

## Lo-fi

Lo-fi makes audible recording limitations or roughness part of the aesthetic.
Tape hiss, room sound, distortion, and imperfect performances can create intimacy.

## Math rock

Math rock uses angular guitar figures, abrupt structural turns, and changing or
unusual meters. Precision and rhythmic interplay often replace a conventional
verse-chorus flow.

## Motorik

Motorik is a steady, propulsive rock beat strongly associated with krautrock. Its
even momentum feels like continuous travel instead of a swinging groove.

## Neo-soul

Neo-soul joins soul singing and groove with influences from hip-hop, jazz, funk,
and modern studio production. Performances often favor behind-the-beat feel.

## Noise pop

Noise pop places memorable pop melodies inside distortion, feedback, and rough
texture. The hook remains central even when the surface sounds abrasive.

## Post-punk

Post-punk expanded punk's economy into angular rhythm, dub space, electronics,
and art-rock experimentation rather than following one fixed sound.

## Shoegaze

Shoegaze uses layered, effects-heavy guitars to form a dense sound field in which
vocals often blend into the texture. The result can feel both loud and dreamlike.

## Slowcore

Slowcore uses restrained tempos, sparse arrangements, and deliberate silence to
make small emotional changes feel large.

## Synthwave

Synthwave imagines cinematic 1980s electronic music through modern production,
often using bright arpeggios, gated drums, and nocturnal imagery.

## Trip-hop

Trip-hop combines hip-hop-derived beats with downtempo pacing, atmospheric
sampling, dub space, and moody vocals.

## Vaporwave

Vaporwave loops and slows fragments of commercial music to explore nostalgia,
consumer imagery, and the uncanny feeling of obsolete digital culture.

## Warm

Warm describes a rounded, comfortable sound with softened high frequencies and
rich low-midrange energy. It is a production or mood descriptor, not a genre.

## Melancholic

Melancholic music suggests reflective sadness rather than necessarily intense
despair. Harmony, timbre, pacing, lyrics, or performance can all create the mood.

## Upbeat

Upbeat commonly means energetic and positive, though it can refer to tempo,
rhythmic lift, mood, or all three. Clarifying which meaning matters improves a
music recommendation.
